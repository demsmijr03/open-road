# Content Intake

Drop everything you have into this folder. **Raw and unstructured is fine.** Loose documents, a
folder of photos, exported social posts, a half-finished brochure. Do not spend time formatting or
organizing it. Structuring is the build's job, not yours.

This folder is **gitignored** so nothing lands in a public repo before it's been reviewed. Vetted
content gets migrated into `src/content/` and committed from there.

## Why this comes before the palette and logo

Design here is derived from subject matter, not applied on top of it. The palette has to live
alongside your actual photography; the logo has to mean something specific to what Open Road does.
If colors get picked first and photos arrive second, the two will fight and the work gets redone.

So: content first, then palette, then logo, then tokens, then pages.

## What to drop in

Anything is better than nothing. Partial content still moves this forward.

| Priority | What | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Photos of real programs and people** | The single biggest driver of the palette |
| **1** | Mission statement + what you actually do | Logo concept and the homepage hero |
| **1** | Legal name, EIN, founding year | Footer, structured data, donor trust signals |
| 2 | Program descriptions | Sitemap and navigation structure |
| 2 | Impact numbers and outcomes | Hero stats, donation framing ("$50 provides X") |
| 2 | Stories, testimonials, quotes | Voice and register, drives the typeface choice |
| 3 | Team and board bios | About page |
| 3 | Existing brochures, annual report, socials | Reveals brand equity you may already have |
| 3 | Pages you think you need | Starting sitemap |

## Two things to flag explicitly

**1. Photo rights.** Nonprofit photography often involves vulnerable people. Tell me which images
you do *not* have clear consent or licensing for, and they will not be published. Settle this now,
not after launch. If you're unsure about an image, say so and it stays out.

**2. Be honest if the photography is weak.** Sparse or low-quality photos are not a problem, but
they change the design direction toward type-led or illustrative work. That decision costs nothing
today and is expensive after the design tokens are frozen. A blunt "we only have a dozen usable
photos" is genuinely useful information.

## Also worth saying out loud

- Organizations whose sites you like, and what you like about them
- Anything you specifically want to avoid
- Colors with existing meaning to your org, or ones that are off-limits
- Accessibility needs specific to your audience (older donors, low-vision users, non-English speakers)
