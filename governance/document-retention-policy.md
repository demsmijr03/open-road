## 1. Why this exists

Form 990 asks whether the organization has a written document retention and
destruction policy. This is that document, and it should be adopted at a board
meeting and minuted so the answer on the return is true.

Beyond the form, it does two practical jobs. It stops records being thrown away
that we later need, and it stops us holding personal information longer than we
have a reason to. Our privacy page makes a public promise about the second, so
this policy is what makes that promise real rather than decorative.

## 2. What it covers

Every record the foundation creates or receives, in any form: paper, email,
files in shared drives, form submissions on the website, and records held on our
behalf by a service provider.

That last category matters more than it sounds. If a form provider stores
submissions in its own dashboard, that copy is ours for the purposes of this
policy and has to be deleted on the same schedule as our own.

## 3. Who is responsible

| Area | Owner |
|---|---|
| Governance records, and this policy | President |
| Financial records, gifts, filings | Treasurer |
| Website enquiries and newsletter data | VP, Marketing |

Each owner is responsible for deleting what has passed its retention period,
and for confirming annually that they have done so. A schedule nobody owns is
the one that quietly lapses.

## 4. Retention schedule

### Governance — keep permanently

| Record | Period |
|---|---|
| Articles of incorporation and amendments | Permanent |
| Bylaws and amendments | Permanent |
| IRS determination letter | Permanent |
| Board and committee minutes | Permanent |
| Conflict of interest policy and signed disclosures | Permanent |
| This policy, and each adopted version of it | Permanent |

### Financial — seven years

| Record | Period |
|---|---|
| Form 990, 990-EZ or 990-N as filed, and supporting records | 7 years |
| Gift and donation records, including donor name and amount | 7 years |
| Receipts and acknowledgment letters issued to donors | 7 years |
| Bank and payment-processor statements | 7 years |
| Scholarship award decisions and disbursement records | 7 years |
| Contracts and agreements | 7 years after expiry |

Seven years is the common practice for records supporting a tax filing. It is
not a single statutory number, and where a specific record has its own
requirement, that requirement wins.

### Personal information from the website — short

| Record | Period |
|---|---|
| Interest form submissions: name, email, role, message | 2 years |
| Newsletter subscriber email addresses | Until they unsubscribe, then delete |

Two years covers two award cycles, September through April, and then stops. It
is a deliberate choice rather than a legal minimum: no sector standard governs
enquiry data, and Pennsylvania has no comprehensive privacy law that reaches
nonprofits. Because nothing forces us to keep it, the honest position is to keep
it only as long as it is doing the job we told people it was for.

**These two periods are published on our privacy page.** Changing them here
means changing them there in the same week.

### Not held by us

Scholarship applications are submitted through the West Chester Area School
District's process, not through this website, and include financial information
this foundation never receives. Nothing in that category is ours to retain or
destroy. If that ever changes, this policy has to be revised before the first
application arrives, not after.

## 5. How records are destroyed

Paper is shredded. Electronic records are deleted from the live system and from
backups where the provider allows it, and from any service provider's own copy.

Deleting an email from an inbox is not deletion if it remains in a trash folder,
an archive, or an export. Whoever owns the area is responsible for the copy they
know about.

## 6. Suspension of destruction

**If litigation, a government investigation, or an audit is underway or
reasonably anticipated, destruction of anything that might relate to it stops
immediately** and does not resume until the President confirms in writing that
it may.

This clause is the most important one in the document. Destroying records to
obstruct a federal investigation is a criminal matter, and it applies to
nonprofits as it does to any organization. A retention schedule is never a
defense for destroying something you had reason to preserve.

## 7. Annual confirmation

Once a year, before the Form 990 is filed, each owner confirms to the board that
records past their retention period have been destroyed, and that no
destruction took place under a suspension. The confirmation is minuted.

## 8. Review

This policy is reviewed annually, and immediately whenever the foundation adopts
a new form provider, donation platform, email service, or anything else that
stores personal information on our behalf.
